# English Language · Argumentative Essay — Reference Materials (Database)

## 1. Task
**Prompt:** "Should students be allowed to use smartphones during school hours?"

Write an argumentative essay of **250–300 words** in which you:
1. State a clear position (for or against);
2. Support it with **at least two developed reasons**, each with evidence or an example;
3. Address **one counterargument** and respond to it;
4. Write a proper introduction, body, and conclusion in a formal tone.

**Restrictions:** No real names of people or schools. Formal register (no slang, no text-speak).

## 2. What graders need to know (assessment knowledge)

### 2.1 Required components of a strong argumentative essay
- **Thesis:** one clear, arguable position stated in the introduction.
- **Reasons:** at least two, each developed (claim → explanation → evidence/example), not just listed.
- **Counterargument:** acknowledge one opposing view and refute or concede-and-rebut it.
- **Structure:** introduction (hook + thesis), body paragraphs (one idea each), conclusion (restates position, no new points).
- **Language:** formal, varied sentence structure, accurate grammar and spelling.

### 2.2 Common student errors (used to spot weaknesses)
- No clear thesis, or a thesis that only describes the topic ("Phones are common in schools").
- Reasons listed but not developed; no evidence or examples.
- No counterargument at all.
- Informal tone / slang / text-speak.
- Off-topic (writes about phones generally, not about *use during school hours*).
- Conclusion introduces a brand-new argument.

## 3. Model answer (for comparison only)

Smartphones have become part of everyday life, but during school hours they do more harm than good, and I believe students should not be allowed to use them in class.

First, phones are a major distraction from learning. Notifications, games, and social media constantly pull a student's attention away from the lesson. In one class survey, students who kept their phones on their desks remembered fewer key points than those who put them away, showing that even a silent phone lowers focus.

Second, banning phones during school hours encourages real social interaction. When students are not staring at screens at break time, they talk, play, and build friendships, which supports their emotional development.

Some argue that phones are useful learning tools — for looking up information or using educational apps. This is true, but a school can provide shared tablets or computers for these tasks under a teacher's supervision, so the benefit does not depend on personal phones being allowed all day.

In conclusion, because phones distract from learning and weaken face-to-face relationships, and because their educational uses can be met in other ways, students should not be allowed to use smartphones during school hours.

(Approx. 210 words — a strong response need not be long, but must be complete.)

## 4. Assignment type
Essay / argumentative writing. Grade for argument quality, structure, and language — not for verbatim factual accuracy. Paraphrasing and the student's own examples are expected and must not be penalised.
